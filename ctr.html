What is CTR?

CTR (Click Through Rate) measures the percentage of users who clicked your advertisement after seeing it. It is one of the most important metrics for evaluating ad performance.

Disclaimer : Enter numbers in your local currency — the calculation works the same regardless of currency.

Formula

CTR = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100

Example

If Clicks = 500
Impressions = 20,000

CTR = 2.5%

How CTR Connects to Your Other Metrics

CTR doesn't exist in isolation — it's usually the first signal in your advertising funnel, and it influences almost everything that comes after it. Understanding how it connects helps you figure out whether a low CTR is really the problem, or just a symptom of something else.

  • CPM (Cost Per Mille) — what you pay per 1,000 impressions, before anyone clicks. A high CPM with a low CTR means you're paying for visibility that isn't converting into interest.
  • CPC (Cost Per Click) — on most auction-based platforms, a higher CTR tends to lower your CPC, since the platform rewards ads it judges as more relevant.
  • CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) — a strong CTR means more people are clicking, but it only helps your CPA if those clicks actually convert. High CTR with a poor CPA usually points to a landing page or targeting mismatch.
  • ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) — CTR tells you if people are interested; ROAS tells you if that interest is actually profitable. The two need to be looked at together, not separately.

In short: CTR is often the earliest warning sign in a campaign. If your CPA or ROAS looks weak, checking CTR first tells you whether the issue starts at the ad itself, or further down the funnel.
