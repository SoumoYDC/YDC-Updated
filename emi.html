What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed amount you pay every month towards your loan repayment. Every EMI consists of two parts:

  • Principal Amount
  • Interest Amount

EMI Formula

EMI = P × R × (1 + R)N ÷ [(1 + R)N − 1]

Where,

  • P = Loan Amount
  • R = Monthly Interest Rate
  • N = Loan Tenure in Months

How Much EMI Can You Afford?

A widely used guideline among lenders and financial planners is the 40% rule — keeping your total monthly EMI payments (across all loans) under 40% of your take-home income. Going beyond this can leave little room for savings, emergencies, or other financial goals, even if a lender is willing to approve a higher amount.

It's also worth remembering that a lower EMI from a longer tenure isn't automatically the "cheaper" option — it usually means paying significantly more in total interest over the life of the loan. Use the total interest and total payment figures above, not just the monthly EMI, to judge the real cost of a loan.

Interest is calculated on your outstanding loan balance, which is highest at the beginning of the loan. As you pay down the principal over time, the interest portion of each EMI shrinks and more of your payment goes toward reducing the balance.
